Executive Summary
HEDIS is the quality-measurement backbone of US managed care. NCQA maintains 90+ measures, and more than 235
million people are enrolled in plans that report them [1]. HEDIS is also the largest data source behind Medicare Advantage
Star Ratings, which drove at least $13.4B in quality bonus payments in 2026 [2]. Most plans still run HEDIS as a spring,
backward-looking batch. It depends on costly medical-record "chart chase" for hybrid measures, and gaps are often found
only after the measurement year closes. Three things make this urgent. NCQA will retire the hybrid method by MY 2029 and
move to digital (FHIR + CQL) HEDIS by about 2030 [9][10][11]. Stars methodology is unstable: 11 measures are being
removed, the equity reward was dropped, and a May 2026 court ruling limited Stars data to HEDIS, CAHPS and HOS [5][21].
And CMS-0057-F FHIR APIs go live in January 2027 [17]. The market is crowded. About 80 organizations appear on NCQA's
measure-certiﬁcation status list [15], and three diﬀerent vendors won Best in KLAS in 2024–2026 [33][34][35]. The real gap
is narrower: helping mid-sized and Medicaid plans become digital-HEDIS-ready and replace hybrid chart chase with year-
round, auditable supplemental data.

1. The Business Problem
1.1 Problem Statement
HEDIS (Healthcare Eﬀectiveness Data and Information Set) is the most widely used performance-measurement framework in
US managed care. NCQA (National Committee for Quality Assurance) develops and maintains it. It has more than 90
measures across six domains: Eﬀectiveness of Care; Access/Availability of Care; Experience of Care; Utilization and Risk-
Adjusted Utilization; Health Plan Descriptive Information; and Measures Reported Using Electronic Clinical Data Systems [1].
Commercial, Medicare and Medicaid plans report HEDIS for NCQA accreditation, NCQA Health Plan Ratings, state Medicaid
contracts and, most importantly, Medicare Advantage (MA) Star Ratings. In MY 2023, NCQA received HEDIS submissions
from 420 commercial, 278 Medicaid and 760 Medicare plan reporting units, 1,458 in total [14].
The money involved is large. MA contracts rated 4 stars or higher get a 5-percentage-point benchmark increase (10 points in
"double bonus" counties). They also keep a larger share of the gap between benchmark and bid as rebates: 65–70% versus
50% for contracts below 3.5 stars [3]. Federal spending on the quality bonus program reached at least $13.4B in 2026, up
from $12.7B in 2025 and more than four times the 2015 level. About 68% of the 35 million MA enrollees are in bonus-
qualifying plans [2]. HEDIS measures are the largest block of clinical-process and outcome measures in the Part C Star Rating,
about 25% of the overall rating weight for MY 2026 [62]. That share will matter more after the 2026 court ruling that CMS
may base Star Ratings only on HEDIS, CAHPS and HOS data (Section 1.3) [21].
Even so, many plans still run HEDIS as an annual, retrospective batch. The measurement year closes on December 31. Plans
then chase medical records for hybrid measures from January to early May, ﬁnish abstraction by early May, and submit to
NCQA by mid-June [28]. By the time a plan ﬁnds that a member with diabetes had no glycemic-status test (GSD measure), or
that a hypertensive member's last blood pressure was 148/92 (CBP measure), the year is over. The quality score, the bonus
money and the chance to get the member recommended care are all lost.
Chart chase is the main cost driver. For hybrid measures, plans draw a systematic sample (historically 411 members per
measure, with oversample) and retrieve records from provider oﬃces [65]. Large plans also run separate retrieval programs
for risk adjustment. Datavant describes one plan initiative that retrieved 2 million charts across 1.5 million members
(vendor-reported) [26]. Public, neutral data on the per-chart cost of payer HEDIS retrieval is scarce. The $15–$50 per chart
range used in BC06 is a common industry rule of thumb, but this research could not verify it. What is published: the HIPAA
ﬂat fee for an electronic patient-requested copy can be up to $6.50, and state fee schedules for other requesters run to caps
of $80–$150+ [27]. The real cost is the retrieval vendor fee plus the labor of credentialed abstractors, which this research
could not quantify from neutral sources.
1.2 Why This Problem Persists
Measure logic is complex and changes every year. Each measure has an initial population (eligible population),
continuous-enrollment rules (typically 12 months with no more than one gap of up to 45 days), required and optional
exclusions (hospice, palliative care, advanced illness and frailty for older members), and numerator logic across several
code systems. NCQA changes measures annually. For MY 2026 it added 7 measures (four risk-adjusted post-surgical
hospitalization measures, DDM, AAF-E and a tobacco-cessation ECDS measure), retired 2 (Asthma Medication Ratio and
Medical Assistance With Smoking and Tobacco Use Cessation), moved four measures to ECDS (LSC, SPC, SPD, and BPD-E
voluntarily), and reissued the whole speciﬁcation in a FHIR-aligned format [8]. In the MY 2027 cycle, NCQA announced
that from MY 2028 it will stop excluding denied claims in 21 measures [10]. Every change means re-engineering the logic
and re-validating it.
The measures BC06 named have themselves changed. The old Comprehensive Diabetes Care (CDC) composite no
longer exists. Diabetes care is now measured as separate measures: Glycemic Status Assessment for Patients With
Diabetes (GSD, which accepts HbA1c or GMI), Eye Exam for Patients With Diabetes (EED), Blood Pressure Control for
Patients With Diabetes (BPD) and Kidney Health Evaluation for Patients With Diabetes (KED) [9][4]. Adult BMI
Assessment (ABA) has been retired. Breast, colorectal and cervical cancer screening are now ECDS measures (BCS-E,

COL-E, CCS-E) [14]. The fact that an internal KANINI document from June 2026 was already out of date shows how
quickly this domain moves.
Data is fragmented, and much of it sits outside claims. One measure can need medical and pharmacy claims, LOINC-
coded lab results, vital signs from EHRs, HIE feeds, case-management registries and provider-submitted supplemental
ﬁles. NCQA's ECDS data hierarchy ranks EHR data ﬁrst, then HIE/registry, then case management, then claims [14]. In a
2025 Arcadia survey, 85% of health plans had not yet centralized their data across systems (vendor-reported) [44].
Annual batch processing ﬁnds gaps too late. A gap found in March for the prior year cannot be closed. Prospective,
year-round review is spreading. For example, Astrata sells "year-round prospective HEDIS" chart abstraction for whole
populations [39]. But many plans still depend mostly on the retrospective season.
Hybrid chart chase does not scale, and it is going away. The hybrid method exists because some clinical facts (a BP
reading, a retinal exam result) were missing from administrative data. NCQA will remove it by MY 2029 [9][10]. Once a
measure is ECDS-only, a chart found in April no longer counts. Evidence must reach the plan as supplemental data
during the measurement period. One vendor estimates this needs "roughly 35x to 75x more supplemental data per
measure" (vendor-reported, not independently veriﬁed) [58].
The rules around the scores keep moving. Tukey outlier deletion in cut-point setting, ﬁrst used for the 2024 Star
Ratings, pushed many cut points up. Earlier CMS modeling had suggested it would lower 16% of contracts by half a star
[64]. The weight of patient-experience measures dropped from 4 to 2 for 2026 [4]. Eleven measures are being removed
from Stars for 2028–2029 [5][7]. The Health Equity Index / "Excellent Health Outcomes for All" reward was cancelled
before its ﬁrst use [5][63]. Plans ﬁnd it hard to forecast which gaps are worth money.
The economics favor incumbents and discourage rip-and-replace. Measure logic must be NCQA-certiﬁed, and results
must pass a HEDIS Compliance Audit by an NCQA-licensed organization. Switching engines in the middle of a season is
risky. So plans stay with incumbents even when those tools are slow or opaque. One consultancy estimates that building
an in-house validated digital engine costs about $1.15M a year fully loaded (not independently veriﬁed) [59].
1.3 Rationale and Industry Context
Bonus dollars are at a record high, but fewer plans get them. QBP spending reached at least $13.4B in 2026 [2]. Yet
only 207 MA-PD contracts (about 40%) earned 4+ stars for 2026, covering about 64% of MA-PD enrollees. The
enrollment-weighted average rating was 3.98, compared with 4.14 in 2023 [4]. Of 516 rated contracts, 175 sit at 3.5
stars, one half-star below the bonus threshold [4]. For these contracts, closing HEDIS gaps is the most direct lever they
control.
Stars litigation has made HEDIS matter more. Humana's challenge over call-center test calls was dismissed with
prejudice in October 2025. Analysts estimated the downgrade could cost Humana more than $1B a year, and the share
of its members in 4+ star plans fell to 20% for 2026 [20]. In Clover Insurance Co. v. HHS (S.D. Ga., May 27, 2026), the
court held that CMS may use only the data types in 42 U.S.C. § 1395w-22(e) (HEDIS, CAHPS and HOS). It invalidated ten
measures, including the Part D medication-adherence measures and call-center measures, and raised Clover's rating
from 3.5 to 4.5 stars, worth about $120M in bonus payments [21]. On June 17, 2026, CMS said it would voluntarily
recalculate ratings for every MA organization on that basis for 2027 QBP [21]. Elevance ($115M claimed), Alignment and
SCAN then sued over the recalculations [22][23]. If the ruling stands, a larger share of each contract's rating will rest on
HEDIS.
The CY 2027 MA/Part D ﬁnal rule (April 2026) shifts Stars toward clinical measures. CMS is removing 11 measures
focused on administrative processes, cancelling the Excellent Health Outcomes for All reward and keeping the historical
reward factor, and adding a Part C Depression Screening and Follow-Up measure starting with MY 2027 (2029 Star
Ratings) [5][6]. Removals include Statin Therapy for Patients with Cardiovascular Disease (from 2028 Stars) and several
complaint, appeals and customer-service measures (from 2029 Stars). Diabetes Care – Eye Exam was proposed for
removal but retained [7].
NCQA has set dates for digital HEDIS. NCQA plans fully digital HEDIS by about MY 2030 and removal of the hybrid
method by MY 2029 [9][11]. The February 2026 update changed some measure-by-measure paths. GSD adds optional

ECDS reporting in MY 2027 and becomes ECDS-only by MY 2029. WCC is to be retired in MY 2029. PPC gets an ECDS risk-

CMS is moving all of its quality programs to digital measures. CMS's Digital Quality Measurement strategy aims to
convert all measures in CMS programs to FHIR-based dQMs. A July 2025 update, "Charting a Path Forward: Transition to
Digital Quality Management", and January 2026 education materials on moving from the eCQM approach to FHIR-based
dQMs followed. FY 2026 payment rules carried "Toward Digital Quality Measurement" provisions [16].
CMS-0057-F builds the FHIR data pipes that digital HEDIS needs. MA organizations, Medicaid and CHIP programs and
managed care plans, and FFE QHP issuers must implement Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer and Prior Authorization FHIR
R4 APIs by January 1, 2027. These APIs share USCDI clinical data plus claims and encounter data [17]. The same
normalized FHIR data a plan builds for these APIs is the input a digital HEDIS engine needs.
Medicaid quality reporting is now mandatory. Reporting the Child Core Set and the behavioral health measures of the
Adult Core Set became mandatory for all states and territories in 2024 [18]. The 2023 ﬁnal rule phases in stratiﬁed
reporting, starting with 25% of mandatory measures for 2025 and 50% for 2026 [19]. Many Core Set measures are
HEDIS-derived, and states pass the reporting burden down to Medicaid MCOs.
Value-based contracts depend on HEDIS-style measures. Risk-bearing provider groups, ACOs and IPAs are paid on
HEDIS-like gap closure across several payer contracts. This is why vendors such as Cozeva (25M+ lives, 84 clients, vendor-
reported) sell into both plans and provider organizations [51], and why Innovaccer and Arcadia sell one quality platform
to payers and providers [43][45].

2. High-Level Objective
2.1 Solution Vision
The application runs HEDIS continuously instead of once a year. It ingests claims, pharmacy, lab, EHR and supplemental data
into a FHIR-normalized member record. It computes HEDIS measures (traditional and digital CQL) on a weekly or daily
cadence during the measurement period. It turns each open gap into a speciﬁc, evidence-backed closure action for outreach
teams, providers and abstractors. It also:
Pulls structured evidence out of unstructured charts (the work the hybrid chart chase does today) year-round, so
evidence counts as supplemental data before the ECDS-only deadline, not as retrospective abstraction afterward.
Estimates what each gap is worth for Star Ratings, NCQA Health Plan Ratings, Medicaid quality withholds and value-
based contracts, and ranks work by value and likelihood of closure.
Prepares the plan for NCQA's digital-only HEDIS by running the FHIR/CQL pipeline next to the incumbent certiﬁed
engine and reconciling the two at member level.
Human-in-the-loop stance. HEDIS is a regulated, audited submission, so the system does not decide compliance for
evidence taken from unstructured records. AI proposes. A credentialed abstractor or quality analyst conﬁrms every chart-
derived numerator hit or exclusion before it enters the supplemental data ﬁle, and every decision has a traceable audit trail
for the HEDIS Compliance Audit. Deterministic measure logic (certiﬁed or validated) computes the rates. AI assists with
evidence retrieval, prioritization and anomaly detection, and never changes the measure speciﬁcation.

2.3 How the System Would Work (Functional Flow)
From the point of view of a health plan HEDIS / Stars quality manager:
1. The quality manager picks the measurement period (e.g., MY 2026) and the programs to track: MA Stars, NCQA Health
Plan Ratings, Medicaid Core Set / state withhold, and provider value-based contracts.
2. The system loads the latest feeds (claims, pharmacy, lab, EHR/HIE, supplemental ﬁles, CMS-0057-F API data), normalizes
them to FHIR, and shows a data-completeness scorecard by source and measure.
3. The system computes all selected measures and shows, for each one, the current rate, denominator, numerator,
exclusions, open gaps, the prior-year cut points, and projected year-end rate at current closure velocity.
4. The quality manager drills into a measure (e.g., CBP), sees the member-level gap list, and ﬁlters it by attributed provider
group, line of business or priority.
5. For each gap, the system shows the missing element, the last relevant event (date, value, source), the recommended
action, and any AI-found evidence waiting for abstractor review.
6. Abstractors work a queue of AI-proposed evidence, viewing the highlighted source passage and accepting or rejecting
each item. Accepted items become supplemental data with full lineage.
7. Outreach and provider-engagement teams get prioritized worklists (member outreach, provider gap reports, point-of-
care alerts). Closures come back as new claims, results or supplemental data.
8. Each week, the system reconciles its results against the incumbent certiﬁed engine (traditional versus digital), ﬂags
diﬀerences at member level, and records a digital-readiness score by measure.
9. At season end, the system produces audit-ready supplemental data ﬁles, the reconciliation evidence and documentation
for the HEDIS Compliance Audit. Submission itself goes through the plan's certiﬁed engine and NCQA IDSS.

The value at stake for buyers is much larger than their software spend: at least $13.4B a year in MA bonuses [2], plus rebate
eﬀects, Medicaid quality withholds and value-based contract incentives. That gap between software cost and value at stake
is why plans pay for software, retrieval and consulting at the same time.

Economic buyer and user. The VP of Quality/Stars or the CMO owns the budget and the Stars P&L. The CIO/CDO owns

acceptance. They need references from similar plans. Pricing is rarely published and is typically per-member-per-month
or enterprise subscription (industry practice; no vendor publishes rates).

3.4 Technology and Adoption Trends
ECDS is now mainstream. In MY 2023, 100% of commercial, 96.8% of Medicaid and 95.1% of Medicare plans submitted
ECDS-based results, up from 50.0% of Medicare plans in MY 2021. NCQA found that plans using non-claims electronic
data generally performed better than claims-only plans [14]. MY 2026 is the ﬁrst full Stars year with Colorectal Cancer
Screening as ECDS-only (vendor-reported) [50].
Digital engine validation has started. Several vendors now market NCQA-validated FHIR/CQL engines. Examples include
Astrata's eMeasure ("one of the ﬁrst NCQA certiﬁed digital HEDIS engines") [39], Smile Digital Health's OmniQ dQM (80
measures, "5 billion data points" in under 12.5 hours, vendor-reported) [41], and Cozeva's "native FHIR digital measure
engines" [51]. A competitor-authored comparison reports that Cotiviti's Quality Intelligence was certiﬁed in June 2026
for all available NCQA digital HEDIS measures, and that Inovalon claims 100% validation coverage for digital measures.
Neither claim was veriﬁed from primary sources [58].
AI abstraction is common among retrieval and abstraction vendors. Astrata claims abstractors work "760% faster" with
NLP-assisted chart review [39]. MRO launched its Prodigy AI abstraction engine in September 2026 [53]. Datavant
acquired AI record-analysis company DigitalOwl in 2025 [47]. Reveleer markets AI-enabled retrieval for HEDIS [38]. All of
these ﬁgures are vendor-reported.
Prospective and point-of-care models are growing. Examples: Innovaccer's EHR-embedded InNote and pharmacy-based
gap closure through EQuIPP [45]; Cozeva's merger with Vatica Health, a point-of-care risk and quality company ranked
#1 by KLAS for three years through 2025 (vendor-reported) [51][52]; Reveleer's bi-directional HIE/EHR integration [38].
Analyst views. KLAS rates the "Quality Measurement & Reporting (Payer)" segment. The Best in KLAS winner changed
every year: Cotiviti Quality Intelligence in 2024 (83.8), Cozeva PayerOne Quality in 2025 (88.1) and Inovalon Converged
Quality in 2026 (88.6) [33][34][35]. This suggests close competition and no dominant leader on customer satisfaction.
IDC's 2025 MarketScape for US Value-Based Healthcare Analytics evaluates HEDIS/Stars reporting modules as core
capabilities [66]. Everest Group's 2025 payer care-management assessment names Stars optimization as a diﬀerentiator
[67]. Detailed KLAS scores and interview commentary are paywalled.
AI adoption in decision-making is still early. In Arcadia's 2025 survey, only 14% of healthcare leaders had fully
integrated AI into decision-making (vendor-reported) [44].

4. Competitive Landscape
4.1 Competitor Categories
1. Full-suite certiﬁed HEDIS / Stars platforms (incumbents): Inovalon, Cotiviti (now including Edifecs), SS&C Health,

5. Provider-side quality reporting (adjacent, not payer HEDIS): Medisolv (hospital eCQM, MIPS, ACO APP; no payer/HEDIS
oﬀering on its site) [54].
6. In-house / DIY: national carriers with their own quality engineering, often on an open-source CQL engine plus NCQA
Digital Content Services [12][13], and systems integrators (e.g., CitiusTech, UST HealthProof, EXL, all on the NCQA list

5.2 Is the Gap Real? (Verdict)
Verdict: crowded market; partial gap by segment and capability.
The core HEDIS calculation is commoditized. About 80 organizations appear on NCQA's MY 2025 measure-certiﬁcation status
list [15]. Four or more vendors market NCQA-validated FHIR/CQL engines [39][41][51][58]. Customer satisfaction is so close
that a diﬀerent vendor won Best in KLAS in each of 2024, 2025 and 2026 [33][34][35]. Retrieval and AI-assisted abstraction
are consolidating fast into well-funded players (Datavant, Reveleer, MRO/Q-Centrix, Cotiviti, Optum) [47][53][58][60].
What is NOT a real gap: - "An automated HEDIS measure calculator" (BC06's original framing). Every certiﬁed vendor already
has one, and without NCQA certiﬁcation it cannot be used for submission. - "In-year / real-time gap lists" and "quality
dashboards". These are standard in Innovaccer, Arcadia, Cozeva, Cotiviti, Inovalon and others [43][45][51][36]. - "AI chart
abstraction" on its own. Astrata, Reveleer, MRO, Datavant (DigitalOwl) and Cotiviti already oﬀer it [38][39][47][53]. - "AI gap
prioritization" as a standalone product. It is a feature, and incumbents can copy it quickly.
Where a gap genuinely remains: 1. Segment: mid-sized regional, Blue and provider-sponsored plans and Medicaid MCOs
(roughly 100K–1M lives). They face the MY 2029 hybrid cut-oﬀ and digital-only HEDIS without the in-house FHIR engineering
that national carriers have, and they are poorly served by multi-quarter enterprise deployments. 2. Capability: the
transition layer between the incumbent certiﬁed engine and digital HEDIS. That means FHIR data readiness (reusing CMS-
0057-F API pipelines), traditional-versus-digital member-level reconciliation, and a human-in-the-loop, audit-traceable

supplemental-data pipeline that turns year-round chart evidence into ECDS-eligible data. Vendors either sell engines (Smile,

5.3 Differentiation Opportunity for KANINI
Where KANINI could credibly win - Wedge: "Digital HEDIS Readiness + Prospective Evidence" program for mid-sized plans.
A ﬁxed-scope engagement plus a reusable accelerator. It (1) maps the plan's claims, lab, EHR and supplemental feeds to FHIR
per the HEDIS Core IG, (2) runs an open-source or partner CQL engine in parallel with the incumbent certiﬁed engine and
reconciles results at member level, and (3) stands up an AI-assisted, abstractor-conﬁrmed supplemental data pipeline for the

engine. Oﬀer the approach with validated engines (e.g., NCQA Digital Content Services [13], Smile [41], Astrata [39]) or the
plan's current vendor. KANINI's value is data engineering, Propel-accelerated build and testing, and operating-model change.
- Audit-ﬁrst AI. Position the AI layer on traceability (source passage, abstractor decision, lineage into supplemental ﬁles)
rather than raw speed. This ﬁts the regulatory stance and answers the main buyer objection. - Stars scenario modeling as a

calendar. Engines are multi-year contracts, and changes cluster in summer and fall. - Domain credibility. Buyers expect
HEDIS-literate staﬀ (certiﬁed abstractors, HEDIS auditor experience) and references. - Crowded messaging. Every vendor
already claims "AI + digital HEDIS". The diﬀerentiation has to show in delivery evidence, such as reconciliation results and
chase-volume reduction, not in positioning.

Appendix A. Data Source and Setup
A.1 Primary Data Source
Synthea synthetic patient data (MITRE). Synthea is an open-source generator of realistic, longitudinal synthetic patients. It
outputs FHIR R4 bundles, CSV tables, C-CDA and optional claims. Records use real terminologies (SNOMED CT conditions,
LOINC labs and vitals, RxNorm medications, CVX immunizations, and ICD-10-CM / CPT / HCPCS on claims), so code-driven
HEDIS logic behaves realistically.
Main site: https://synthea.mitre.org/ · Downloads (pre-generated sets): https://synthea.mitre.org/downloads
Source and wiki: https://github.com/synthetichealth/synthea · https://github.com/synthetichealth/synthea/wiki
FHIR for Research overview: https://mitre.github.io/fhir-for-research/modules/synthea-overview
Relevant contents: patients , encounters , conditions , observations  (HbA1c, BP, eGFR, uACR), procedures
(mammography, colonoscopy), medications , immunizations , claims /claims_transactions , payers  and
payer_transitions  (enrollment spans for continuous-enrollment logic), plus optional clinical notes for NLP/abstraction
demos.
A.2 Data Generation / Acquisition Process
1. git clone https://github.com/synthetichealth/synthea.git  and cd synthea .
2. Generate a reproducible population: ./run_synthea -p 100000 -s 12345 Massachusetts  (repeat for 2–3 states for
geographic spread).
3. Key settings in src/main/resources/synthea.properties : - exporter.fhir.export = true  and exporter.csv.export
= true  - exporter.years_of_history = 10  (colonoscopy has a 10-year lookback) -
exporter.practitioner.fhir.export = true , exporter.hospital.fhir.export = true  (provider attribution) -
exporter.clinical_note.export = true  (unstructured notes for the AI abstraction layer) -
generate.only_alive_patients = false  (tests death and hospice exclusions)
4. Load FHIR bundles into a FHIR server (e.g., HAPI) for the digital path, and CSV into a relational store for the traditional
path.
5. Download value sets from VSAC (UMLS licence) and CQL libraries for overlapping eCQMs from the eCQI Resource Center
(A.4).
A.3 Use-Case-Specific Data Requirements
Population: at least 100,000 members across 2–3 states with 3+ years of history for measure logic; a 10-year history for
COL-E lookbacks.
Target measure set (current names, MY 2026): GSD (Glycemic Status Assessment for Patients With Diabetes), EED (Eye
Exam for Patients With Diabetes), BPD (Blood Pressure Control for Patients With Diabetes), KED (Kidney Health
Evaluation for Patients With Diabetes), CBP (Controlling High Blood Pressure), BCS-E (Breast Cancer Screening), COL-E
(Colorectal Cancer Screening), CCS-E (Cervical Cancer Screening), SPD (Statin Therapy for Patients With Diabetes), AAB
(Avoidance of Antibiotic Treatment for Acute Bronchitis/Bronchiolitis). Replaces BC06's list: CDC was split into
GSD/EED/BPD/KED; ABA was retired.

/ 8462-4), mammography, colonoscopy and FIT events, and retinal exams (EED coverage in Synthea may be thin; inject if
needed).
Enrollment: derive coverage spans from payer_transitions  and inject 30–45-day gaps into 5–10% of members to test
continuous-enrollment logic.

The calendar period a measure evaluates. From MY 2026, NCQA uses "measurement period" in FHIR-
